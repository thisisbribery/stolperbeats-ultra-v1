Stolperbeats ULTRA flasher

Alt swing tables, ratchets, MIDI pitch and fill mode, added to your own copy of the official Stolperbeats firmware. See, hear and make your own Timings first in the STOLPERBEATS VISUALISER.

🔒 Runs entirely in your browser. Your firmware is never uploaded, and this site hosts none.

1Load your official firmware

Use the unmodified _MSM_stolperbeats_hw_rev4-163.bin from Making Sound Machines. Other versions and already-modded files (including Firmware lab builds) are refused, because every patch targets exact addresses.

2Swing tables on the Timing buttons

Put any table on any of the four Timing buttons: the factory ones, the alt tables from the visualiser, your own Timings from the visualiser's CREATOR, or a .timing.json you upload. Each button keeps its other behaviour (accents, Rolls). The picture shows where the 8 steps of a half bar land at Shuffle 1 to 6.

Make your own in the visualiser ↗

3Choose your mods

Mix them freely. Fill CV needs Fill mode. All four share one engine that is added once; switching a mod off leaves its controls exactly as stock.

4Build and download

The code mods are checked against the expected SHA-256 for your exact combination before anything is written. Then your tables go on top.

Recipes: share your picks, never the firmware

A recipe is a small .json with your table picks, the rows of any custom tables, and your mods. Anyone can open it here with their own official file and build the same firmware. The visualiser's Firmware lab recipes open here too.

5Flash it over USB

Chrome or Edge on a computer can flash the module straight from this page (WebUSB). Anywhere else, use dfu-util the way the manual describes.

  1. Connect the module to your computer with USB, rack powered.
  2. Hold BOOT, press and release RESET, then let go of BOOT. The module is now in its bootloader.
  3. Click Connect and pick STM32 BOOTLOADER (or DFU in FS Mode).
  4. Click Flash my build. The page erases only the sectors it needs, writes, reads everything back to check it byte for byte, then restarts the module.
sudo dfu-util -a 0 -s 0x08000000 -D your-build.bin

The STM32 bootloader lives in the chip's ROM, so a bad flash can always be undone: do BOOT and RESET again and flash the official file.

How it works

The page holds only the added code, the places where the firmware calls into it, a few switches inside it, and the timing-table values the visualiser shows. No part of the official firmware is included. After your file passes the version check, the mods are written into a copy in memory and checked against the expected SHA-256 for your exact combination, then your tables are written on top.

What's been tested

Everything on this page has been tested on my own Stolperbeats: all five alt tables, ratchets, pitch, fill mode and the fill CV gate. With all four mods on, the code is byte for byte the build that runs on my module, and with Push = DEEP and Clave = SQEZ the whole file is identical to it.

The only thing not tried on hardware is picking and choosing: a build with only some of the mods. Those builds use the same tested code, with nothing new added. Leaving a mod out puts the firmware's own original call back where the mod would hook in, or flips a small switch in the engine that skips that mod's part. Every combination was checked by running the module's own code in a Cortex-M7 emulator: ratchet hits, MIDI notes, Fill on and off, the CV 6 gate, and fills that play the same in every build.

Known differences when mods are installed

With Ratchets: the Shuffle encoder sets ratchets in Pattern Edit instead of changing the shuffle. With Pitch: Tap in Pattern Edit shows the step's pitch instead of tapping the tempo. With Fill: Linear + Tap switches Fill mode, and while Fill mode is on, tap tempo is off (the tempo encoder still works). Everything else plays exactly like the firmware it was built on.
